### 2.3 · Video 3 — Client Dashboard / AI Messaging Validation (0:26, 23s · 45 words)
> "Now we're on the client's dashboard. The client asks Aruga AI to explain the home program in simpler terms. Still unsure, they send it to their therapist for verification. The therapist reviews the program and sends clear feedback back. No guessing — the clinician confirms it."

### 2.4 · Video 4 — Creating the Home Program (0:49, 29s · 65 words)

Enter the number of sessions and the frequency, and the practice schedule fills itself automatically. Then we add the activities for this program. What starts as a blank form quickly becomes a structured week of practice — and the therapist can adjust anything before assigning it to the client."

*Say "fills itself automatically" exactly as the auto-fill happens on screen.*

### 2.5 · Video 5 — Creating with the Activities Agent (1:18, 16s · 27 words)

### 2.6 · Video 6 — Client Clarifies (1:34, 11s · 25 words)
> "The client receives the program and wants to clarify one step. They attach the program, so the therapist reviews exactly what they mean before replying."

### 2.7 · Video 7 — Doing the Home Program (1:45, 25s · 45 words)
> "The client starts the home program at home. They open the worksheet, draw over it, interact with the tasks, add notes, and save. They can also add videos of the activity, and record how it felt. Then they submit everything for their therapist to review."

### 2.8 · Video 8 — Therapist Feedback + Biomechanical Agent (2:10, 47s · ~107 words)
Three beats, matching your edit: feedback choice → biomech output → SOAP (~24s) + send.

> **[feedback screen]** "Back on the therapist view, it's time to give feedback. The therapist can write text feedback — or use the Biomechanical Agent for an advanced analysis of the submitted video."
>
> **[biomech overview/output on screen]** "Here is the output. The movement, tracked frame by frame. Joint angles and range of motion, measured. Each movement flagged Good or Watch, with a plain-language reason. Instead of eyeballing the video, the therapist sees evidence."
>
> **[over the SOAP notes, ~24s]** "Those measurements flow into the clinical notes — subjective, objective, assessment, and plan — each part grounded in what the agent measured and what the client reported. The therapist edits, confirms every word, then sends the feedback and the next steps back to the client."

**Talking about the output — the only improvisation point:** if one flag or angle is clearly readable on your screen, pause on it and read its label verbatim (e.g. *"This one is marked Watch — [reason shown on screen]."*) in place of the generic "Each movement flagged…" sentence. Read **one** insight aloud; let the rest be seen. Advantages to land, in the script's own words: *evidence instead of eyeballing* (biomech beat) and *notes grounded in measurements, not memory* (SOAP beat).

## 3. Script Rules (what was fixed)

- **One pass, in screen order.** Creating → doing → feedback/biomech happens once, as edited; the script doesn't preview or repeat it.
- **Every line matches its visual.** Nothing is described before it appears ("fills itself automatically" lands on the auto-fill; "Here is the output" lands on the results, not the launch).
- **No mention of the client's phone or device** anywhere in VO or captions — doing the program is *"at home."*
- **Claim-safe wording:** the agent *explains, reviews against evidence, measures, and drafts*; the therapist *checks, publishes, confirms, edits, and sends*. No "diagnoses / advises / treats" · "AI therapist" · "clinical-grade" · "replaces therapy."
- **Word budget:** ~370 spoken words total ≈ 2:33 of speech inside 2:57 (verified per clip, §2) — the remainder is your transitions and visual breathing room. Don't add filler to close those gaps.
- Captions burned in; every angle/flag the VO names also appears as on-screen text (muted judges).
